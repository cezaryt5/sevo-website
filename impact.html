From Pavements to Permanence.

The Problem

For the women of Nyala's local markets, business used to be a gamble, not an investment. Trading "on the ground" meant operating in a legal gray area.

  • Asset Seizure — Inventory frequently confiscated during municipal sweeps.
  • Legal Fines — Operating in the streets led to recurring fines that swallowed daily profits.
  • Exposure — No shelter meant being at the mercy of the weather.

The Solution

Through a strategic partnership with local banks and the municipality, we designed and manufactured fixed metal kiosks — official workplaces that provide dignity and weather protection.

  • Physical Infrastructure — Fixed metal kiosks as designated official workplaces.
  • Legal Recognition — Women shifted from "illegal vendors" to "authorized traders."
  • Financial Fuel — Integration into microfinance programs for business expansion.

The Impact

Today, the green kiosks represent more than just a place to sell — they represent a permanent stake in the local economy. By solving the root cause, we've built a framework where women can finally plan for the future.

Vocational Training

The 2012 Genesis: Stitching a Social Fabric.

The 30-Woman Blueprint

In 2012, we launched a pilot program that proved economic empowerment is the most effective tool for peace-building. We curated a micro-society:

  • 10 women from Dorkti (El Geneina, West Darfur)
  • 10 women from Al Salam Refugee Camp
  • 10 women from Nyala

By placing women from different backgrounds in the same room, we gave them a rare chance to share experiences and realize their struggles were identical.

From Skill to Scale

Under the guidance of UN-Habitat and UNHCR, these women mastered tailoring, specializing in traditional Sudanese thobes. To ensure sustainability:

  • Asset Ownership — Every woman was equipped with her own professional sewing machine.
  • Business Autonomy — They left as business owners with capital to start their own workshops.

Water & Sanitation

Clean Water Wells Initiative.

The Water Crisis

In rural Sudan, access to clean water is a daily struggle. Women and children walk hours each day to fetch water, often from contaminated sources.

  • Waterborne Diseases — Cholera, typhoid, and dysentery are common.
  • Lost Education — Children, especially girls, miss school to fetch water.
  • Economic Burden — Hours spent fetching water could be used for productive activities.

Our Solution

We've built 27 clean water wells across rural communities, providing safe, accessible water to over 15,000 people.

  • Community Training — Local committees trained to maintain and manage the wells.
  • Hygiene Education — Communities receive training on proper water usage and sanitation.
  • Sustainable Design — Wells built to last, with durable pumps and protective infrastructure.

Health & Education

Mobile Health Clinics.

Healthcare Desert

Remote communities in Sudan lack access to basic healthcare facilities. Many villages are hours away from the nearest clinic.

  • Preventable Diseases — Vaccination rates are low, and preventable diseases spread easily.
  • Maternal Mortality — Women lack access to prenatal and postnatal care.
  • Untreated Conditions — Common illnesses go untreated, leading to complications.

Mobile Health Solution

Our mobile health clinics bring medical care directly to remote communities, providing free consultations, vaccinations, and health education.

  • Primary Care — Treatment of common illnesses and injuries.
  • Vaccinations — Immunization programs for children and adults.
  • Maternal Health — Prenatal care, safe delivery support, and postnatal checkups.
